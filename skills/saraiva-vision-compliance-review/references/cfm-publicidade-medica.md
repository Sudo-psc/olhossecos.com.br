# CFM publicidade medica review checklist (summary)

This is a practical review checklist, not legal advice. Always confirm with the
current CFM resolution and local CRM guidance before final publication.

## Professional identification

- Include full name and CRM + UF for any medical professional mentioned.
- List only specialties officially registered for the professional.

## Claims, guarantees, and superlatives

- Avoid promises of cure or guaranteed outcomes.
- Avoid superlatives (best, only, unique, definitive) unless explicitly allowed
  and supported by approved documentation.
- Avoid statements that can be read as medical advice without context.

## Pricing, discounts, and promotions

- Avoid publishing prices, discounts, or promotional bundles for medical acts
  unless current rules explicitly allow the format.

## Before/after and comparative content

- Avoid before/after images or subjective comparisons unless clearly permitted
  by current CFM rules and written patient consent is documented.
- Avoid comparative claims against other professionals or clinics.

## Patient data and consent

- Do not publish identifiable patient images or data without explicit consent.
- Prefer anonymized, educational content over case advertising.

## Testimonials and social proof

- Avoid inducement or sensational testimonials.
- If testimonials are used, require written consent and keep them factual.

## Devices, procedures, and approvals

- When referencing devices or procedures, ensure they are approved for use
  (e.g., ANVISA) and avoid experimental claims.
- Do not imply exclusivity or superiority without documented authorization.

## Tone and framing

- Prefer educational, informational language over promotional language.
- Highlight risks and limitations when relevant.

## Escalate for legal review when

- Content includes clinical outcome claims.
- New advertising formats are introduced (ads, landing pages, paid media).
- Patient images, testimonials, or case studies are used.
